How the treasury, governance, and project financing actually work. Tokenomics covers the fee split and mint parameters — this page covers what happens after a fee is collected: custody, the four governance layers, how a project actually gets funded, and why Solana was chosen over the alternatives.

Treasury & custody

The treasury is Aretia's catalyst fund — a standing, continuously replenished pool, not a one-time pledge or a grant round that depletes and has to be re-raised. Every trade tops it up again by protocol design, whether or not anyone is actively fundraising.

Custody

Accumulated fees settle into a Squads multisig — Solana's equivalent of Gnosis Safe — requiring multiple independent signers to approve any outbound disbursement. No single key can move treasury funds. Live on mainnet at 3FyoJdvC7Fa...N6cyzZg, 2-of-3 threshold, real independent co-signers, with the full propose-approve-execute cycle already tested end to end.

Two forms of disbursement

Grants — a lump-sum transfer to a vetted project partner not itself tokenized, such as a reforestation cooperative or community solar developer. Retirements — the treasury buys and permanently retires tokenized carbon credits on-chain, producing a provable, quantified tonnes-CO2 figure tied to a transaction hash.

Governance — four layers, not one vote

Flat "holders vote on every payout" governance breaks the moment a treasury is large enough to be worth attacking — a single well-organized proposal can extract real money from a naive vote. ACT governance runs in four layers instead.

  • 01

    Community

    Token holders, via Realms / SPL Governance, vote on funding priorities, project categories, and treasury allocation weights — not individual project payouts.

  • 02

    Technical committee

    Domain experts screen individual project applications against climate-science credibility, MRV soundness, financial feasibility, and social/environmental risk.

  • 03

    Independent verification

    A party independent of both the protocol team and the project developer confirms a funded project actually did what it claimed, before further milestone funds release.

  • 04

    Smart-contract execution

    Funds move only after Layers 2 and 3 have both signed off on a given milestone. No human discretion is exercised at the point of transfer itself.

Governance is phased in, not switched on at launch: the treasury runs on team-curated grants first, with Layer 1 voting handed over once there's a large enough, engaged holder base for it to be meaningful rather than symbolic.

Milestone financing

Approved projects don't receive their full commitment as one lump sum.

Capital releases in tranches tied to verified progress: an approval commits a total amount, then each tranche releases only after independent verification (Layer 3) confirms the prior milestone was actually met. This is the structural mechanism that keeps "the treasury financed a project" from silently meaning "the treasury sent money to a project that never finished."

Project portfolio

"Tied to many climate-friendly projects" means the treasury spreads across categories rather than committing to one. Each category carries its own diligence bar — none of this is decided by the smart contract; it's a curation and governance question that sits entirely off-chain.

Why Solana

The deciding factor is native fee-on-transfer support, not raw transaction cost. Without it, the withholding mechanism would have to be bespoke program logic, written and audited specifically for this project instead of relying on infrastructure the entire ecosystem already uses correctly.
