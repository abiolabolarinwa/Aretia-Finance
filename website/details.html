The Economics Behind the Mechanism

Not a forecast: the arithmetic and the incentives.

Why the fee doesn't obviously kill trading, why the commitment is credible rather than cheap talk, and what the fund would actually generate at different levels of volume.

Fee incidence

Does a 3.5% fee meaningfully deter trading?

It's a real cost, and no trading history yet exists to measure the answer empirically. Two things are still worth noting. First, transfer fees in this general range aren't unusual among comparable Solana Token-2022 assets that have achieved meaningful volume; the fee alone isn't obviously prohibitive at this scale. Second, and more specific to this design: the fee is fixed and fully disclosed before any transaction, unlike slippage or price impact, both of which vary with trade size and pool depth and are frequently larger in absolute terms than the fee itself. A rational trader prices a known, fixed cost into their decision the same way they'd price any other one.

Mechanism design

Why "code-enforced" is a different claim than "we promise"

Economics has a name for the difference: cheap talk versus a credible commitment device. A stated intention the issuer could costlessly reverse, such as "we'll donate a share of proceeds," is cheap talk regardless of how sincerely it's made. A property enforced by an immutable, permissionless token standard, verifiable by anyone without trusting the issuer's account of it, is a credible commitment in the technical sense: the cost of reneging isn't just reputational, it's architectural, since reneging would require rewriting a mint configuration that no longer has an authority capable of rewriting it. That's what permanently revoking mint authority actually buys, at the cost of the immutability tradeoff described in the tokenomics section above and the whitepaper.

Whitepaper §12.2

Liquidity

The catalyst fund only works if someone provides liquidity

Whoever eventually supplies the other side of the ACT-USDT pool bears impermanent loss, a standard, well-documented property of automated market making, not a feature specific to this token. It's worth stating plainly because the fund's continuous replenishment depends on trading volume, and trading volume depends on liquidity existing in the first place, which in turn depends on someone accepting that risk in exchange for a share of trading fees. As of this writing, no liquidity pool has been seeded yet; an initial, self-funded seed is planned, sized to deepen gradually rather than launch at full depth immediately. See the roadmap.

Aretia Finance / Treasury

Treasury & custody

The treasury is Aretia's catalyst fund: a standing, continuously replenished pool, not a one-time pledge or a grant round that depletes and has to be re-raised. Accumulated fees settle into a Squads multisig, live on mainnet at GtKGE6mQRj...bryZnA, with the full propose-approve-execute cycle already tested end to end.

Custody threshold 2 of 3 signers approve every disbursement
Treasury

Two forms of disbursement.

Grants: a lump-sum transfer to a vetted project partner not itself tokenized, such as a reforestation cooperative or community solar developer. Retirements: the treasury buys and permanently retires tokenized carbon credits on-chain, producing a provable, quantified tonnes-CO2 figure tied to a transaction hash.

Governance: four layers, not one vote

Flat "holders vote on every payout" governance breaks the moment a treasury is large enough to be worth attacking: a single well-organized proposal can extract real money from a naive vote. ACT governance runs in four layers instead.

  • 01

    Community

    Token holders, via Realms / SPL Governance, vote on funding priorities, project categories, and treasury allocation weights, not individual project payouts.

  • 02

    Technical committee

    Domain experts screen individual project applications against climate-science credibility, MRV soundness, financial feasibility, and social/environmental risk.

  • 03

    Independent verification

    A party independent of both the protocol team and the project developer confirms a funded project actually did what it claimed, before further milestone funds release.

  • 04

    Smart-contract execution

    Funds move only after Layers 2 and 3 have both signed off on a given milestone. No human discretion is exercised at the point of transfer itself.

Governance is phased in, not switched on at launch: the treasury runs on team-curated grants first, with Layer 1 voting handed over once there's a large enough, engaged holder base for it to be meaningful rather than symbolic.

Milestone financing

Approved projects don't receive their full commitment as one lump sum.

Capital releases in tranches tied to verified progress: an approval commits a total amount, then each tranche releases only after independent verification (Layer 3) confirms the prior milestone was actually met. This is the structural mechanism that keeps "the treasury financed a project" from silently meaning "the treasury sent money to a project that never finished."

Fee & funding questions.

How much is the transfer fee, and where does it go?
350 basis points (3.5%) on every transfer, withheld automatically by the Token-2022 standard itself, not by any custom logic Aretia wrote. Of that: 96.5% of the original transfer reaches the recipient net; 2% funds the climate catalyst fund; 1% goes to the liquidity pool; 0.5% is a disclosed management fee that funds Aretia Finance LLC's operational costs of running the treasury. There is no burn allocation. Full breakdown in the Tokenomics section above.
Can the team just decide not to route the fee where it says it will?
No, that's the specific distinction this project draws against tokens whose charitable commitments are a stated intention rather than a property of the code. The fee split is enforced by the mint's own on-chain configuration; there's no code path in which a transfer completes without it, and no policy step for anyone to skip.
How does a project actually get funded?
Never as a single lump sum. An approved project's total commitment is divided into tranches, and each tranche releases only after independent verification confirms the prior milestone was actually met. See the milestone financing and governance sections above for the full lifecycle.
Do token holders vote on which projects get funded?
Not directly, and not yet. Governance is layered rather than flat, specifically to avoid the failure mode where a well-organized proposal can extract funds from a naive one-holder-one-vote system. Token holders (Layer 1) set funding priorities and category weights, not individual payouts; a technical committee screens applications; independent verification confirms milestones; smart-contract execution releases funds only once both have signed off. Layer 1 activates once the holder base is large enough for a vote to be meaningful; until then, the treasury runs on team-curated grants under the same milestone-verification discipline.
Can I apply for project funding?
Yes, the application intake is live. See Apply for Funding. Submissions are received and reviewed; automated scoring and milestone-gated disbursement are still being built (see the roadmap), so expect a real review period rather than an instant response.
