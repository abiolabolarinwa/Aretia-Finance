---

When you use an exchange account, the company holds the assets and you hold a claim on them. When you use a self-custody wallet, you hold a secret (a recovery phrase) that controls the assets directly on the blockchain, and the wallet software is only a way to use it. The difference is about who can say no.

## What you gain with self-custody

- **No one can freeze or withhold your assets.** Not a company, not a court order served on a company, not an outage on a withdrawals page.
- **No counterparty risk from the app.** If the app's company fails, the assets in your wallet are still yours, because they never sat on the company's books.
- **Access to everything on the chain.** You can use any decentralised exchange, send to any address and connect to any application that supports your wallet.

## What you take on

- **You are the recovery plan.** If you lose the recovery phrase, no support team can restore it. If someone else gets it, they can take everything and nothing reverses it.
- **You are the one who signs.** A transaction you approve is final. Approving a malicious request can drain a wallet, so reading what you sign matters.
- **You pay network fees yourself** and choose the network, and sending to a wrong address or the wrong network can lose funds.

## Habits that reduce the risk

1. Write the recovery phrase on paper, never in a photo, a note app or a chat. Store it somewhere you control.
2. Never type it into a website or give it to anyone, including anyone claiming to be support.
3. Use a separate wallet with a small balance for experiments and unfamiliar sites.
4. Read the request before you sign. If a request asks for permission to spend all of a token ("unlimited approval"), consider whether you need it.
5. Test with a small amount first when you use a new network or address.

## It is not all or nothing

Many people hold most of their assets in self-custody and keep a smaller amount on an exchange for convenience, or the reverse. The honest question is which risks you are better placed to manage: a company's, or your own.
