Stake ACT. Build your access tier.

Lock ACT for a chosen duration and the protocol computes a deterministic Capital Access Score from the amount and time committed, a number anyone can verify on-chain rather than a promise anyone has to trust. It's the mechanism proposed in Section 30 of the whitepaper for prioritizing access to Aretia's proposed second capital stage. The program that computes it is live and tested on Solana devnet right now, while ACT isn't trading yet: the least disruptive time to get the infrastructure right before it ever touches mainnet.

Why stake ACT

Staking doesn't change what ACT does today: the transfer fee, the catalyst fund, and the treasury all work exactly as documented whether or not a single wallet ever stakes. What it adds is a way to start building standing now, before there's anything to have standing for.

Priority when Stage Two exists

If the proposed project-finance marketplace in Part VIII of the whitepaper is ever built, staking tier is the proposed mechanism for who sees a project's data room first, not who gets to invest, just who gets to look first.

Commitment is rewarded, not just size

A longer lock earns a higher duration multiplier on top of the amount staked. Someone who commits ACT for a year builds a materially higher score per token than someone locking for a month.

Computed on-chain, checkable by anyone

Your score isn't a number in a database Aretia controls. It's stored in an account on Solana that any wallet, block explorer, or script can read directly, the same self-verification principle behind the mint checker on the Verify page.

Early participation compounds

Because duration is part of the score, staking now, while there's no trading pressure and nothing urgent competing for the same ACT, builds age credit that a wallet staking the same amount later simply won't have yet.

Staking & Access

The more ACT you stake, the earlier you see what's investable.

Projects that complete a full catalyst-fund cycle and clear underwriting are proposed to graduate into a separately instrumented investment opportunity, never routed through ACT itself. Staking tier decides who sees that opportunity first and in the most depth, not who's allowed to invest: eligibility to actually commit capital always depends on the holder's own jurisdiction and that instrument's own rules, never on ACT.

What happens when you stake

01

Choose amount & lock

Pick how much ACT to stake and one of four lock periods. Topping up an existing, still-locked position blends its age fairly rather than resetting the clock for free.

02

ACT moves to the vault

Tokens transfer to a program-controlled vault, subject to the same transfer fee as any other ACT transfer. What's credited is the actual net amount received, not the amount you sent.

03

Score & tier compute automatically

CAS = amount × duration multiplier. The resulting tier is cached on your stake account, readable directly from the chain by any wallet or explorer.

04

Withdraw once the lock matures

Unstaking is only possible after the chosen lock period ends, and the return transfer is subject to the transfer fee like any other ACT movement.

What staking does not do Read this part

Two rules from Section 30 of the whitepaper apply to this program without exception, and will keep applying no matter how the tier system is refined later.

1

No tier confers investor eligibility. A higher stake or a longer lock changes what a wallet can see and how early. It never changes whether that wallet is legally permitted to invest in anything. That's governed entirely by securities law in the holder's own jurisdiction, independent of anything staked.

2

ACT's value is never tied to a gated project's returns. Staking ACT can grant visibility into a project's numbers. It never entitles a holder to a share of that project's cash flows, and ACT's own marketed value must never be represented as depending on how any gated project performs.

Also true today: Stage Two, the marketplace this tiering was proposed to gate, does not exist. No project has completed a full catalyst-fund cycle. Staking right now builds a real, on-chain, verifiable score; it does not currently unlock a data room, a listing, or an investment opportunity, because none exist yet. See Section 32 of the whitepaper for the five conditions that would need to be true before that changes.

Staking questions.

Can I stake ACT yet?
Not with real ACT. The program is compiled and live on Solana devnet, and locking/unlocking has been tested there with real transactions, but it has not been deployed to mainnet and hasn't been independently reviewed yet. No real ACT can be staked today.
What does staking actually do?
Locking ACT for a chosen duration computes a Capital Access Score from the amount and time staked, which maps to a tier. The tier is proposed to gate priority visibility into a possible future project-finance marketplace (Part VIII of the whitepaper), a marketplace that does not exist yet, so staking today builds a verifiable on-chain score and unlocks nothing.
Does staking pay a yield, or give me investor rights in a project?
No, and this is a hard rule rather than a current limitation: no staking tier confers investor eligibility, and ACT's value is never represented as depending on any gated project's returns. Staking changes what a wallet can see and how early, never what it's entitled to. See Section 30 of the whitepaper.
